How the census works

Where the figures come from, what counts as an active player or a new character, and how main scenario progress is measured.

Data collection

The FFXIV Census indexes public character profiles from the official Square Enix FINAL FANTASY XIV: The Lodestone.

Rate-limited background workers scan character ID ranges to discover new characters and refresh known profiles on a schedule. Every request follows strict rate limits and backs off politely, so the census never strains the Lodestone.

Profile visibility

Players can make parts of their Lodestone profile private from their Square Enix account: the whole profile, achievements, login activity, minions, mounts and more, each on its own. When the profile page is hidden, the census cannot read anything about that character, and the character is left out of every figure. When only achievements are private, the census still records the name, world, race and job levels, but activity and main scenario figures for that character are incomplete.

Active players

Square Enix does not publish login figures or subscription counts, so the census estimates activity from public achievements within a rolling 30-day window.

A character counts as active when their globally latest public achievement was earned in the last 30 days. This is an achievement-based signal, not a measure of logins or subscriptions.

Each refresh reads the latest achievement from one achievement-history request. Missing story milestones are then checked one by one in release order, stopping at the first one the character has not earned.

Characters can play without earning achievements. A character counts as inactive when their latest public achievement is older than 30 days or their achievements are private, so this figure never claims activity the public achievement history cannot show.

New characters

Earlier versions counted a character as new on the day the census first found it. That date can mislead: older or returning characters are often found during routine sweeps.

The new characters figure, and every 30-day timeline, instead dates a character by when they earned an early main scenario milestone:

Achievement ID 590, “My Little Chocobo”
Earned for receiving a company chocobo from the player’s Grand Company (the Maelstrom, the Order of the Twin Adder or the Immortal Flames) around level 20 of the main scenario.

Characters without this achievement, or whose achievements are private, are left out of the new characters timeline. Every counted character has played past character creation and into the story, rather than being an indexing artefact.

Grand Companies

A character’s Grand Company is read from their public Lodestone profile: the Maelstrom, the Order of the Twin Adder or the Immortal Flames. A profile that names no company counts as No Grand Company, a group of its own, so the shares always add up to 100%.

Each company’s active share is how many of its members are active players, using the same 30-day achievement signal as everywhere else. The race lean compares races across the whole game.

The figures follow characters as they are first found or removed throughout the day. A character who changes Grand Company after being counted keeps their old company until the nightly rebuild, which recounts every character. See the Grand Companies for the current figures.

Jobs

Job levels are read from each character’s public Lodestone profile. A job counts as levelled at level 1 or more, and at the level cap when it has reached the game’s current maximum level.

The Lodestone shows a base class and its job as one entry, so a base class counts towards its job: a Gladiator counts as a Paladin, a Conjurer as a White Mage, and so on. An Arcanist counts towards Summoner, the job that shares its level on the Lodestone, and not Scholar. Nothing is counted twice.

Blue Mage is a limited job with its own lower cap, so it counts as at the level cap at level 80. It is left out of the party balance, because it cannot join ordinary duties. The party balance counts jobs, not characters: a character with two tanks at the cap counts twice.

Job figures are rebuilt nightly, and the Jobs page says when they last were. While older characters are still being prepared for job counting, the page says how many characters the figures cover. See Jobs for the current figures.
