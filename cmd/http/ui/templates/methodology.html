How the census works

Where the figures come from, what counts as an active player or a new character, and how main scenario progress is measured.

Data collection

The FFXIV Census indexes public character profiles from the official Square Enix FINAL FANTASY XIV: The Lodestone.

Rate-limited background workers scan character ID ranges to discover new characters and refresh known profiles on a schedule. Every request follows strict rate limits and backs off politely, so the census never strains the Lodestone.

Profile visibility

Players can make parts of their Lodestone profile private from their Square Enix account: the whole profile, achievements, login activity, minions, mounts and more, each on its own. When the profile page is hidden, the census cannot read anything about that character, and the character is left out of every figure. When only achievements are private, the census still records the name, world, race and job levels, but activity and main scenario figures for that character are incomplete.

Active players

Square Enix does not publish login figures or subscription counts, so the census estimates activity from public achievements earned in the last {{$.ActivityWindow.Long}}.

A character counts as active when their globally latest public achievement was earned in the last {{$.ActivityWindow.Long}}. This is an achievement-based signal, not a measure of logins or subscriptions.

Why {{$.ActivityWindow.Long}}? A full census pass takes months. The census revisits characters in turn, so a character’s latest achievement is only as recent as the census’s last visit. A short window would miss players the census has not revisited yet. A wide window catches more of them, but while a pass takes longer than the window, the figure still undercounts active players.

Each refresh reads the latest achievement from one achievement-history request. Missing story milestones are then checked one by one in release order, stopping at the first one the character has not earned.

Characters can play without earning achievements. A character counts as inactive when their latest public achievement is older than {{$.ActivityWindow.Long}} or their achievements are private, so this figure never claims activity the public achievement history cannot show.

New characters

Earlier versions counted a character as new on the day the census first found it. That date can mislead: older or returning characters are often found during routine sweeps.

The new characters figure, and its daily timeline, instead dates a character by when they earned an early main scenario milestone:

Achievement ID 590, “My Little Chocobo”
Earned for receiving a company chocobo from the player’s Grand Company (the Maelstrom, the Order of the Twin Adder or the Immortal Flames) around level 20 of the main scenario.

A character counts as new when they earned this achievement in the last {{$.NewCharactersWindow.Long}}, up to the last full day in UTC. The trend compares that count with the {{$.NewCharactersWindow.Long}} before.

Why {{$.NewCharactersWindow.Long}}? The census sees a new chocobo only when it next visits the character, and a full pass takes months. So the most recent days are always undercounted. A wide window evens this out, but while a pass takes longer than the window, the figure still undercounts new characters.

Characters without this achievement, or whose achievements are private, are left out of the new characters timeline. Every counted character has played past character creation and into the story, rather than being an indexing artefact.

Grand Companies

A character’s Grand Company is read from their public Lodestone profile: the Maelstrom, the Order of the Twin Adder or the Immortal Flames. A profile that names no company counts as No Grand Company, a group of its own, so the shares always add up to 100%.

Each company’s active share is how many of its members are active players, using the same signal as everywhere else: a public achievement in the last {{$.ActivityWindow.Long}}. The race lean compares races across the whole game.

The figures follow characters as they are first found or removed throughout the day. A character who changes Grand Company after being counted keeps their old company until the nightly rebuild, which recounts every character. See the Grand Companies for the current figures.

Free Companies

A character’s Free Company is read from their public Lodestone profile. A company’s members are the members seen by the census: the characters it has found in that company. It is not the official roster, which only the Lodestone shows, so a company whose members the census hasn’t reached yet looks smaller than it is.

A member is active after earning a public achievement in the last {{$.ActivityWindow.Long}}, the same signal as everywhere else. Members who hide their achievements can’t count as active, so active counts are a lower bound.

Each board ranks the top 100 companies of a world, datacenter, region or the whole game, by members or by active members. A company needs at least 2 members seen to be ranked. Ties go to the company with more of the other measure, then by name. A company belongs to one world, so the census keeps each world’s top 100 for both boards and builds every wider board from those lists. When companies tie on both counts at a world’s 100th place, the name decides, as it does everywhere on the board (up to 20 tied companies per world are considered; beyond that, the lower Lodestone ids).

The boards are rebuilt nightly, and say when they last were. A company’s name comes from one of its members the census has seen, so a company that has been renamed shows its new name once the census has visited its members again. The census lists companies only: no member lists and no individual characters. See the Free Company rankings for the current boards.

Jobs

Job levels are read from each character’s public Lodestone profile. A job counts at any level once it is level 1 or more, and at the level cap when it has reached the game’s current maximum level.

The Lodestone shows a base class and its job as one entry, so a base class counts towards its job: a Gladiator counts as a Paladin, a Conjurer as a White Mage, and so on. An Arcanist counts towards Summoner, the job that shares its level on the Lodestone, and not Scholar. Nothing is counted twice.

Blue Mage is a limited job with its own lower cap, so it counts as at the level cap at level 80. It is left out of the party balance, because it cannot join ordinary duties. The party balance counts jobs, not characters: a character with two tanks at the cap counts twice.

Job figures are rebuilt nightly, and the Jobs page says when they last were. While older characters are still being prepared for job counting, the page says how many characters the figures cover. See Jobs for the current figures.

Achievement privacy

Players can hide their achievements on the Lodestone and keep the rest of their profile public. The census puts every character in one of three groups:

Public
The census can read the character’s achievements: the latest one and the story milestones.
Private
The character hides their achievements. The census still records their name, world, race, Grand Company and job levels.
Not yet checked
The census hasn’t read the character’s achievements yet. This is the census’s backlog, not the player’s choice: once checked, the character moves to public or private. A public profile with no achievements at all also stays here.

A private character never counts as active and never shows as finishing an expansion, so the active-player and main scenario figures are a lower bound. Population, race, Grand Company and job figures are unaffected.

The privacy counts are rebuilt nightly. Through the day they follow characters as they are first found or removed; a character who hides or shows their achievements after being counted moves at the next nightly rebuild. The most and least private worlds leave out any world with fewer than a quarter of the median world’s characters, so a handful of characters can’t top a list. See Private achievements for the current figures.
